# PRESCIENT threat model (ForecastLedger v1.2)

Scope: contracts/src/ForecastLedger.sol, the keeper, the agent toolkit and the web app. Every item names the defence and the test that checks it (contracts/test). Status as of 23 Nov 2026: implemented and tested (65 tests, 100% coverage, see SECURITY.md); not deployed; independent review pending.

## Assets
* The integrity of each forecaster's track record (commits, reveals, scores).
* The integrity of the Agent ID Crowd forecast and the market baseline.
* Users' wallets: the contract never holds or moves assets and asks for no approvals.

## Trust assumptions (disclosed)
* Predict.fun resolution goes through its own UMA style oracle with managed proposers. Each of the three mainnet CTFs resolves through a different oracle contract. We read payouts from the chain and never pick outcomes.
* The keeper (ROUND_MANAGER) chooses questions by the published selection rule and posts the market baseline. It cannot change any Brier score, any outcome, any commit or any reveal. It does set the baseline, and therefore every forecaster's skill for that round (corrected after the security review, finding L1). Limits: the baseline is posted in clear, once, only in the last 15 minutes before the commit deadline, before any reveal or outcome exists, so it cannot be withheld or chosen with hindsight; the raw order books are archived publicly; operator wallets are never ranked.
* One Agent ID per Binance user is enforced by Binance, not by us.

## Threats and defences
1. Copying other forecasters. Commits are keccak256 hashes over a 32 byte random salt, so a low entropy probability vector cannot be brute forced. Test: hash binding fuzz.
2. Copying the market. Skill is measured against the market baseline, so a market price copied at the moment of the baseline snapshot scores about zero skill. Residual (delta review): the baseline is public onchain from the moment it is posted, and commits stay open until the deadline, so a forecaster who copies the market later books skill equal to the price move inside the window: bounded by the 15 minute contract window, about 5 minutes with the keeper target. Accepted and disclosed. Test: score with baseline.
3. Selective reveal. A committer who does not reveal gets forfeit: every non void question counts as Brier 1e8 (the worst possible), with skill against the baseline computed the same way. Not revealing is never better than revealing, per address (an identity holding two addresses is covered in item 5 and residual risk R4). Exception: a round nobody seals in time (residual risk R1). Test: fuzz "forfeit never beats any reveal".
4. Cherry picking rounds. Off chain leaderboard rules: minimum 5 rounds and 20 scored questions, participation rate shown, ranking on shrunk mean skill.
5. Many identities (Sybil). Commit requires an Agent ID (one per Binance user). Residual risk: a person with two Binance accounts. Disclosed.
6. Replay across chains or contracts. The hash includes COMMIT_TYPEHASH, chainid, the ledger address, roundId and the forecaster. Test: hash binding fuzz.
7. Hash collisions. abi.encode only, never abi.encodePacked.
8. Front running a reveal. revealFor binds the forecaster in the hash, so anyone submitting the reveal can only submit the same forecast for the same forecaster. Test: revealFor by a third party.
9. Grief by spam. No loop over forecasters onchain; question loops are bounded by 16; batch functions cap at 100 and skip already processed entries. Test: batch skip.
10. Malicious external contract. Agent ID and up to 4 CTF addresses are immutable, checked for code at deploy, and only view functions are called. None of the mainnet targets has an EIP1967 proxy slot (checked 23 Nov 2026).
11. Oracle risk. Splits are Void. Questions still unresolved after the reveal deadline plus the round's resolution wait can be voided by anyone, and only if the chain still shows them unresolved. Nobody can set Yes or No by hand.
12. Admin abuse. Roles: ROUND_MANAGER, PAUSER, DEFAULT_ADMIN (two step transfer with a 1 day delay). Pause blocks only createRound and commit; reveal, finalize, seal, score and forfeit always work. No upgrade path, no selfdestruct, no delegatecall, no payable functions. Test: pause never blocks reveal or score.
13. Baseline manipulation (changed in v1.2). The round manager posts the baseline vector in clear with setBaseline, once, only in the last 15 minutes before the commit deadline (contract enforced), before any reveal or outcome exists. This removes the hindsight veto of the old commit and reveal baseline (review M1) and the early or stale baseline (the early half of review L4; the last look half is bounded, see item 2). The manager still sees who committed (not what) when it posts; the admin can still grant itself the round manager role or revoke the keeper inside the window (standard role powers, visible onchain, decided blind). The values are still chosen by the manager (review L1): the raw order books are archived publicly, the indexer flags a round without a baseline (baseline_missing), and operator wallets (round manager, admin, pauser) are never ranked. A round without a baseline scores with skill 0 for everyone; that is decided blind, before the deadline. Tests: test_baseline_*, test_missingBaseline_scoresSkillZero.
14. Timestamp manipulation. Windows are hours long on mainnet (immutable minimums of at least 2 h commit and 12 h reveal, enforced by the constructor on chain 56; each at most 7 days); BSC blocks are subsecond. Test networks may use minute windows for fast end to end runs.
15. Lost salt. The CLI stores the salt locally (mode 0600) and prints a reveal receipt. Without the salt the forecaster forfeits. Users are warned.
16. Prompt injection. Market titles and all API or onchain text are untrusted data; the skill never follows instructions found in them.
17. Front end and supply chain. Pinned dependencies and lockfiles, strict CSP, no secrets in the client, Predict.fun key server side only, a pre commit secret scan, dependabot alerts.
18. Early resolution leak (added 23 Nov 2026). A market can resolve while commits are still open (for example "Will X happen by date" when X happens early). Defences:
   * finalizeQuestion before the commit deadline voids the question;
   * the round is sealed at or after the commit deadline (sealRound, called by the keeper right after the deadline, or automatically by the first reveal or finalize): every question already resolved onchain at that moment becomes Void;
   * finalize after the deadline on an unsealed round seals first.
   Residual risk: a market that resolves after the deadline but before the seal is voided (neutral for everyone). The keeper seals right after the deadline (docs/KEEPER.md). Information that leaks without an onchain resolution is handled by ranking on skill versus the market (docs/SCORING.md). Test: early resolution cases.
19. Burned or reissued Agent ID (Owais decision, 23 Nov 2026). Agent ID ownership is checked only at commit. reveal and revealFor require only the commit hash, so losing the Agent ID between commit and reveal never forces an unfair forfeit. Test: commit, burn, then reveal and revealFor succeed.
20. Scoring order. The baseline status is final at the commit deadline (setBaseline is refused from then on), and score and forfeit run only after the reveal deadline, so every forecaster in a round is scored against the same baseline. Test: score before the deadline reverts.
21. Profile names. Opt in only. setPublicProfile checks ownerOf(keccak256(label)) == caller, and setPublicProfile(false, ...) always clears the name (test_profile_optOutClearsName). No lookup of wallets by name for people who did not opt in (red line 9).
22. Malformed CTF return data. A CTF that returns fewer than 32 bytes would make a decoding call revert even inside try/catch. _seal and voidStale use a length checked staticcall instead, so reveals and voiding can never be blocked this way. Tests: test_malformedCtfData_*.
23. Per round resolution wait (added 23 Nov 2026, ledger v1.1). The round manager picks the wait at createRound, between 1 and 120 days (7 days for daily rounds), so long horizon event rounds are possible. Risks and defences:
   * A short wait lets unresolved questions be voided sooner. Bounded below by 1 day, published in RoundCreated before any commit, and readable with getRound, so forecasters see it before committing; it can never change afterwards. Voiding is neutral for everyone in the round.
   * A long wait delays scoring for that round only; scoring of other rounds is independent, and pause still cannot block anything.
   * Tests: contracts/test/ResolutionWait.t.sol (bounds, storage and event, 7 day and 120 day voidStale timing).
24. Baseline timing and liveness. The contract accepts setBaseline only in [commitDeadline minus 15 minutes, commitDeadline). A missed window is permanent: nobody, the admin included, can post later, and the round scores skill 0 for everyone (delta review: the outage budget is the 15 minute window). The keeper targets 5 minutes before the deadline, retries inside the window over two RPC endpoints, and the watchdog and the seal job alert on a missing baseline; keeper/lib/baseline.mjs refuses a snapshot outside the window, including when slow fetches push it past the deadline. Tests: keeper/test/baseline.test.mjs and the contract window tests.
25. Admin transfer (review L8, fixed in v1.2). When a two step admin transfer completes, the old admin's PAUSER_ROLE moves to the new admin, so a retired admin key cannot pause. Other pauser holders are untouched. Tests: test_adminTransfer_movesPauserRole, test_adminTransfer_leavesOtherPausersAlone.

## Accepted residual risks (not solved; Owais decision 23 Nov 2026)
* R1 (review L5) Lazy seal. If nobody seals during the reveal window, the first finalize, however late, seals then and voids every question already resolved, so a lone non revealer escapes the forfeit and a lone committer holds a free option. Mitigations, not a fix: the keeper seals at the deadline (prescient_seal.timer, 23:00:20 UTC) and every 15 minutes (tick); the watchdog alerts on a missing seal 15 minutes after the deadline; anyone can call sealRound.
* R2 (review L6) Ordering around the seal. Between the commit deadline and the first seal, a question that resolves onchain is voided; after the seal it counts. Whoever lands the first seal decides for a question resolving in that window, which is a selective void, not a neutral one. Mitigations: the keeper seals within seconds of the deadline; selection prefers markets that do not resolve right after the deadline.
* R3 (review L7) voidStale timing. After the reveal deadline plus the round's resolution wait, anyone may void a still unresolved question at a moment of their choosing, which is not neutral per forecaster. Mitigations: daily rounds use 7 days; the keeper never calls voidStale while the market's resolution is pending (PRICE_PROPOSED or PRICE_DISPUTED) and finalizes promptly.
* R4 (review L2, L3) One identity, two addresses. If an Agent ID can be burned or reissued to another wallet inside a commit window, one person can hold two commits in a round, or restart a record. The real Agent ID has no standard burn, revoke or reclaim function, but has an owner with admin functions not identified, so this is unverified; asked to the organizers. Mitigation: the indexer publishes identityChanged for any committer whose Agent ID left their wallet after the ledger went live.
